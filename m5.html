Module 5: Risk rules, sizing and the dip-buy pause

Diagnose first. Before reading: what are 1%, 2%, 8% and 25% of your account in dollars? Write them down.

The course rules

One name no more than 25% of the account. All options no more than 35%. Keep at least 8% in cash, outside any put collateral. Any new idea risks no more than 1–2% of the account, written in dollars before the order. Stop opening new options if the account falls to your floor.

Worst case vs stop

A put's worst case is strike minus premium, times 100. A stop is your plan to exit earlier, but gaps and overnight moves can blow through it. Know both numbers.

Volatility and time

Higher implied volatility means bigger premiums because bigger moves are expected. Time decay (theta) works for the seller, fastest in the last few weeks. Big premiums before earnings come with big gap risk.

The dip-buy pause

Before buying into any drop: Does it fit the caps? What is the max loss in dollars? Would I make this trade if I did not already own it? If any answer is shaky, wait 24 hours.

Worked example

Illustrative: on a $100,000 account, 1–2% is $1,000–$2,000, 25% is $25,000, and 8% is $8,000. A single $50 put has a $4,900 worst case, far above 2%. That means you would need a cheaper stock, a smaller account share, or a clearly agreed exception before trading it.
