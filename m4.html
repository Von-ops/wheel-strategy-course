Module 4: Assignment and the full cycle

Diagnose first. Before reading: after a put is assigned, what do you own? After a call is assigned, what is left?

The loop

Cash, then a put. If assigned, shares, then covered calls. If called away, back to cash. You can also stop at any point: closing a trade or sitting in cash is always allowed.

Assignment is not a failure

It is the promise being kept. Stock options can be exercised early, especially when they are deep in the money near expiration or right before a dividend. Have the cash or shares ready from day one.

After assignment

Recheck the company, your caps and your cash before selling a call. Never sell a call below your breakeven just to collect a bigger premium, because if it is assigned you lock in a loss.

Rolling in a cash account

Rolling means closing one option and opening another. In a cash account you may not be able to do both in one order, and new trades need settled cash. Treat every roll as a new trade that has to pass the rules.

Worked example

Illustrative: assigned at $50 after a $1.00 put premium, so your breakeven is $49. You sell a $52 call for $0.75 and the shares are called away. Total profit: $200 on the shares + $100 + $75 in premiums = $375 before costs. If instead XYZ sits at $42 and the call expires, you are still down about $625 overall.

Teach it back

Applied scenario

Your shares are below breakeven and someone offers a fat premium for a low-strike call. State the loss assignment would lock in before deciding.
