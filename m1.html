Module 1: Why the wheel, and what LEAPs teach you

Diagnose first. Before reading, answer out loud: what has to happen for a bought call to make money? How is selling an option different?

Buying options vs selling them

When you buy a call, you need the stock to move up enough, soon enough, to beat what you paid. Direction, timing and price all have to go your way, and the option can expire worthless. When you sell an option, you take the other side: you collect a payment now in exchange for a promise to buy or sell 100 shares at a set price.

What the wheel is

The wheel is two promises used in a loop. First you sell a cash-secured put, promising to buy 100 shares at a strike you would be happy paying. If you end up buying them, you sell covered calls, promising to sell those shares at a higher strike. If they get sold, you are back to cash and can start over.

What it is not

The premium is payment for real risk, not free money. If the stock collapses, you still own the drop. If it soars, your upside is capped at the call strike. The wheel is a slower, rules-based income tool, not a way to win back losses fast.

The lesson from long-dated calls

A far out-of-the-money LEAP is mostly hope with a deadline. Following someone else's trades copies their entries but not their cash, exits or risk limits. A call worth 40% of what you paid needs a gain of 150% just to get back to even, so the price you paid should never be the reason to keep holding.

Worked example

Illustrative math: you paid $1,000 for a call that is now worth $400. To get back to $1,000 it must rise 150%. A 50% bounce only gets it to $600. Judge the next dollar of risk on its own merits.

Teach it back

In your own words: why can a cheap call still be too big a position, and why does collecting premium not make a wheel trade safe?

Applied scenario

A stock you like drops 12% and you feel the urge to buy more calls. Run the three-question pause before you even look at prices: Does it fit the caps? What is the max loss in dollars? Would I make this trade if I did not already own it? If any answer is shaky, wait 24 hours.
